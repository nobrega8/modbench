\chapter{Troubleshooting}

\section{No answer (timeouts)}

\begin{description}[style=nextline, leftmargin=0pt, font=\intermedium]
  \item[On a serial line] Check, in this order: the unit ID; baud rate,
    parity and stop bits (\ui{Find…} tries them,
    Section~\ref{sec:find-devices}); the A and B wires, which are often
    swapped; the termination at both ends of a long line; and that no other
    master talks on the same line.
  \item[On the network] Ping the address; check the port (502, or the
    converter's); some devices accept one client at a time, so close other
    programs connected to it. Through a gateway, the unit ID selects the
    device behind it.
  \item[Slow devices] Raise \field{Timeout}, and add a \field{Delay} between
    requests. Statistics suggests a timeout from the measured answers
    (Section~\ref{sec:statistics}).
\end{description}

\section{Values look wrong}

\begin{description}[style=nextline, leftmargin=0pt, font=\intermedium]
  \item[Off by one register] The manual counts from 1 and the map, or your
    request, from 0, or the other way round. Try the address one lower or
    higher, or check \val{address\_base} in the map.
  \item[Huge or tiny numbers] Usually the byte or word order. Try the
    \field{Byte order} override in step 2, or paste the registers into the
    Data decoder (Section~\ref{sec:data-decoder}).
  \item[Wrong by a factor of 10, 100\ldots] The scale. Maps give it per register;
    some devices also have a scale factor register.
  \item[A mismatch banner] The device is not the one the map is for, or its
    firmware differs (Chapter~\ref{ch:maps}).
\end{description}

\section{Bad frames}

A CRC or LRC error means the bytes arrived damaged: noise on the line, a
missing termination, a wrong baud rate, or two devices answering at once
because they share a unit ID. The Raw data tab shows the frame
(Section~\ref{sec:raw}).
